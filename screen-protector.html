Screen protectors are the best expected-value purchase in phone ownership — a $12 pack against a $300 screen repair — and simultaneously the aisle with the most identical-looking products in electronics. The differences that matter are material, case fit, sensor compatibility, and above all the install system. This guide covers each honestly, including which marketing claims are furniture. Glass vs. film: pick by phone, not preference

Tempered glass is the default for flat and gently curved phones: it feels like the factory screen, resists keys and sand, and — its real job — cracks so your screen doesn't, absorbing drop impact and peeling off in defeat. Thickness runs 0.2–0.5mm; thinner feels better, thicker survives more, and either beats nothing by a mile.

Film (TPU/PET) adds nearly zero thickness, conforms to curved edges, and self-heals fine scratches — it's the mandatory answer on folding screens (glass doesn't fold) and the better answer on aggressively curved panels where glass lifts at the edges. It doesn't help much in drops; pair it with a good case and treat it as scratch armor.

Privacy and matte variants trade brightness for their feature: privacy filters dim the screen noticeably and narrow viewing angles (that's the point), matte kills glare and fingerprints but softens sharpness slightly. Both are honest trades — just know you're making them.

Decoding the claims: 9H, oleophobic, and "military grade"

"9H hardness" is a pencil-scale rating every tempered glass sheet clears — it's the floor, not a feature; a listing leading with it has nothing better to say. Oleophobic coating is where quality actually varies: the fingerprint-shrugging slickness of good glass wears off cheap glass in weeks — "got grippy/smudgy after a month" reviews are the tell. Touch accuracy and clarity separate tiers too: bargain glass can add typing lag and a faint haze; brand-name sheets don't. "Military grade" and hammer-demo videos are theater — the hammer spreads force exactly the way a sidewalk corner doesn't. And edge quality matters more than any demo: cheap glass chips at the rim from pocket contact, which is how protectors die of old age rather than heroism.

Case fit and sensors: the two compatibility traps

The case war: a full edge-to-edge protector and a thick-lipped case fight for the same millimeter, and the case wins by peeling the corner — the single most common complaint in the category. Fixes: "case-friendly" sizing (slightly inset from the edges), or buying case and glass from the same brand line, which is boring and works. If you run a rugged case, its raised bezel already does half the protector's job — thin glass is plenty. (Case-mount logic continues in the car mount guide, where thickness fights magnets instead.)

The sensor question: in-screen ultrasonic fingerprint readers want thin, explicitly-rated glass, and want fingerprints re-registered after install — that one step resolves most "broke my sensor" reviews. Optical readers and face unlock tolerate nearly anything. Search reviews for your phone model plus "fingerprint" before ordering; thirty seconds, zero returns.

The install is the product

The biggest improvement in this category in a decade isn't glass — it's the alignment tray: a plastic jig that positions the sheet so you can't miss. Buy protectors that include one; the skill has been engineered out. The rest of the ritual still helps: install after a hot shower (steam settles dust), clean twice with the included wet/dry wipes, chase the final speck with the dust sticker, then commit in one motion. Pressure bubbles squeegee to the edge; dust bubbles are permanent tenants — which is why multipacks (2–3 sheets) are the correct format: sheet one is practice, sheet two is forever, sheet three is next year's refresh when the coating wears.

When to buy

Protectors are perpetual lightning-deal fodder with real lows on Prime Day and Black Friday, but the calendar matters less than the phone cycle: buy the protector before or with the phone — the naked-phone window is when screens crack, and day-one glass costs the same as day-90 glass. FAQ

Glass or film?

Flat/gently curved phones: glass. Foldables and sharp curves: film. Drops: glass. Scratches only: either.

Is 9H meaningful?

Every tempered sheet claims it — it's the floor. Judge coating longevity and edge quality instead.

Will it fight my case?

Edge-to-edge glass + thick lip = peeled corner. Buy case-friendly sizing or matched brand lines.

Bubble-free install?

Alignment tray, steamy room, double clean, dust stickers, one confident lay. Multipacks forgive.

Fingerprint sensor issues?

Ultrasonic readers want rated-thin glass and re-registered prints. Optical readers don't care.
