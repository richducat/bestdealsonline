Blackout Curtains: The Fabric Is Half the Job — Hanging Is the Other Half

Blackout curtains have a unique review pattern: five stars from people who hung them generously, one star from people who hung the same fabric exactly window-width and met the sunrise anyway. Both reviews are accurate — because darkness is a system of fabric plus coverage, and listings only sell the fabric. This guide covers both halves. True blackout vs. room-darkening: the flashlight test

True blackout fabric — usually triple-weave or foam-backed — passes essentially no light: held against a bright window, you see black. Room-darkening blocks most light but glows amber at high sun; fine for living rooms and light sleepers, wrong for night-shift workers and nap-dependent toddlers. Listings use the terms interchangeably, so verify with two checks: search the reviews for "can still see light" (its presence or absence tells you everything), and trust customer photos taken at noon over any studio image. Foam-backed fabrics block slightly more but drape stiffer and can't be ironed; triple-weave drapes like a real curtain and washes normally — the mainstream pick.

The light-gap problem: where the sunrise actually gets in

Fabric that blocks 100% still fails around its own edges. The leaks, in order: over the top (rod mounted at frame height), down the sides (panels cut exactly window-width), and the center split. The fixes are hanging decisions, made before you order:

  • Mount high and wide: rod 4–6" above the frame and 8–12" past it each side. The wall overlap is what seals the sides.
  • Buy fullness: total panel width 2–2.5× the rod span. Stretched-flat panels leak and look like bedsheets; folds seal and look intentional.
  • Wraparound (return) rods curve the fabric back to the wall at each end — the single best $10 upgrade for genuine darkness.
  • Kill the center split with overlapping panels, magnetic strips sewn into leading edges, or simply ordering one extra-wide panel for smaller windows.

For total-darkness cases — night-shift sleep, nurseries with a 5am problem — the endgame is blackout plus: a cheap tension-rod liner inside the frame, or side-track systems that channel the fabric. But try generous hanging first; it solves most bedrooms alone.

Thermal and sound claims, honestly

The density that blocks light also traps an air layer at the glass, so blackout curtains genuinely moderate temperature: noticeably less summer heat gain on west windows, milder winter drafts on old single-panes. It's a real effect with a modest ceiling — supplemental, not insulation. Sound claims are weaker: heavy fabric takes the edge off echo and high-frequency street noise, but physics gives thin fabric no chance against bass and traffic rumble; buy them for light and treat quiet as a bonus. If summer heat is the primary mission, light-colored or white-backed exteriors reflect better than dark faces — you can have a navy room-side and a white street-side, and your heating and cooling both work less.

Measuring and styles, quickly

Measure from the future rod position (high and wide), not the window: that's the number the width math runs on. Length: floor-grazing (within ½") in bedrooms and living rooms; sill-length over radiators and in kitchens. Grommet tops slide easiest for daily use; rod pockets look soft but drag; pleated headers on rings draw beautifully and read custom. Panels are sold singly and in pairs — check which, twice; it's the category's most common ordering error, right ahead of forgetting that "84 inches" means the panel, not the coverage.

FAQ

Blackout vs. room-darkening?

Blackout passes nothing at noon; room-darkening glows. Search reviews for "can still see light" and read the photos.

Why does light still get in?

The edges, not the fabric. Mount high and wide, buy 2× fullness, wraparound rods, overlap the center.

Do thermal claims work?

Moderately and genuinely — drafts and heat gain shrink. It supplements insulation, doesn't replace it.

What size?

Width 2–2.5× the rod span; floor-length in living spaces. Measure from the rod position, not the window.

Which header style?

Grommets for daily sliding, pleats for looks and draw, a valance over either to seal the top gap.
