Bluetooth Speaker Buying Guide: Size Is the Spec That Matters

Bluetooth speakers are marketed on everything except the thing that predicts how they sound: physical size. Drivers move air; air is bass; small boxes can't cheat that, whatever the wattage sticker claims. Once you accept the size-sound trade, the rest of the decision is battery honesty, water ratings, and whether you'll ever want two of them playing together. Pick your size class first

Pocket / clip-on (soap-bar size): shower soundtracks, handlebar audio, solo podcasts on a hike. Clear vocals, no meaningful bass, gloriously portable. Buy for convenience, not for music quality.

Compact (water-bottle size): the category's center of gravity — real midrange, the first hints of honest bass, still fits a backpack side pocket. Right answer for desks, kitchens, picnics, and dorm rooms.

Mid-size (loaf-of-bread): fills a living room or backyard patio, produces bass you feel slightly rather than just hear. The point where "speaker for the trip" becomes "the house speaker that travels."

Party class (small duffel): genuinely loud outdoors, real low end, often with light shows and mic inputs. Heavy, priced accordingly, and overkill indoors — these exist for gatherings, garages, and job sites.

Battery claims: divide by two outdoors

Printed battery hours are measured at moderate indoor volume — roughly half volume, no bass boost. Crank it outdoors and consumption balloons: the honest planning number is about half the rating at party volume. Details that matter more than the headline hours: USB-C charging (retire the micro-USB stragglers — one cable ecosystem, see the cable guide), a charge-level readout better than one blinking LED, and a power-bank output on bigger speakers, which turns the beach speaker into phone insurance. Battery care mirrors power banks: hot cars kill cells, and storing at half charge beats storing empty.

Water ratings, decoded once

IP codes read as dust-then-water: IPX7 means untested for dust but survives 30 minutes fully submerged — pools, rain, showers covered. IP67 adds a full dust seal, which is the beach-and-trail spec worth insisting on for outdoor speakers. IPX4 is splash-only: kitchen safe, pool risky. And a marketing phrase like "waterproof design" with no IP number belongs in the same bin as "military grade" — if the listing doesn't print the rating, assume it wouldn't pass one. Floating models add real value for pools and boats; salt water remains everyone's enemy, rated or not — rinse after.

Features that matter, features that don't

Worth having: multipoint (speaker holds connections to two phones — whoever grabs it next just plays), stereo pairing within a brand, an aux-in jack on home speakers (old amps, turntables, and airport TVs still exist), and speakerphone quality if it'll live on a desk. Mostly noise: wattage numbers (unregulated marketing in this category — a "40W" no-name routinely loses to a 20W brand unit), EQ apps you'll open once, and LED light rings unless the light show is honestly the point, which at parties it can be.

One structural note: stereo pairing and party modes only work within a brand. If you can imagine owning two speakers someday, you're choosing an ecosystem today — the same quiet lock-in as charging ecosystems, and worth deciding deliberately.

Situation shortcuts

Dorm: compact class, IPX5+, multipoint for roommate harmony — one of the safest picks on the dorm essentials list, since speakers are allowed where cooking gear isn't. Shower: pocket class with a clip or suction mount and IPX7; cheap is correct here, steam and drops happen. Backyard and grilling: mid-size, IP67, and set it high — waist-height placement outdoors is half the "why is it quiet" complaint. Travel: compact, USB-C, under a pound; the hotel-room upgrade nobody regrets packing. Gifts: compact class from a known brand in a fun color is the highest-hit-rate audio gift there is; include the receipt anyway, because speaker taste is personal.

FAQ

Why does my small speaker sound thin?

Bass is moving air, and air needs driver size and cabinet volume. Size honestly predicts sound; wattage stickers don't.

IPX7 vs. IP67?

Both survive a dunk; IP67 adds full dust sealing. No printed IP rating means assume none.

Are battery claims real?

At half volume, yes. At outdoor volume, plan on half the printed hours.

Can two speakers play together?

Same brand: usually. Cross-brand: no. Your first speaker quietly picks your second.

Bluetooth or smart speaker?

Plugged-in rooms: smart. Everywhere else: Bluetooth. Many homes correctly own one of each.
