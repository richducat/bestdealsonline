### Text post — r/Frugal or r/personalfinance-adjacent (value-first, link optional)

> **Title:** The 10-second routine that's saved me from every "50% OFF!!" trap this year
>
> After getting burned twice on "deals" that turned out to be the everyday price with a fancy
> badge, I started running the same four checks before buying anything online. Takes ten seconds:
>
> 1. **Ignore the strikethrough price.** Ask what the thing actually sold for last month. If you
>    don't know, a price-history lookup answers it instantly.
> 2. **Ask if the sale is rare.** If it's "40% off" every other week, that's not a sale — that's
>    the price wearing a costume.
> 3. **Check the final cart price**, coupons and all. Coupons on quietly-raised prices are a
>    classic.
> 4. **Read the 3 newest critical reviews.** Merged listings mean the star average might belong to
>    an older version of the product.
>
> The countdown timer is theater — decide what it's worth *before* you look at the clock.
>

### Comment template — someone asks "is this a good deal?"

> Quick way to sanity-check it: look up the product's price history first (what it's sold for in
> the last ~3 months matters way more than the % badge). If today's price is a real drop from its
> *typical* price — not its list price — and it doesn't drop this low every other week, it's a
> genuine deal. If it's basically at its usual price with a red badge on it, wait.
